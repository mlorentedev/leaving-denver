# Lesson: Use Real Urgency, Not Invented Scarcity, in a Moving Sale

## Context (The Problem/Error)
The first copy leaned on direct-response tropes: "everything must go", "moving abroad in 3
weeks", tax-inflated "you'd pay" math, and the idea of announcing that prices will go up.

## The Finding (Root Cause/Solution)
On peer-to-peer marketplaces these backfire:
- **They are checkable lies.** Moving-sale prices only go down, and Facebook shows "price
  dropped" on every cut.
- **"Leaving the country soon, must go"** is the script scammers use, and it draws lowballers.
- **False claims to sell** can count as a deceptive practice under consumer-protection law. For the
  car, a false claim also gives the buyer recourse after the sale.

Honest urgency does the same job: the real departure date (2026-11-09) with a countdown, "no holds —
first to arrive with payment", sold items kept visible, an "X of 14 sold" counter, bundles that
close when a piece sells, and "was $X, now $Y" after a real drop. Keep the drop schedule private,
because publishing it teaches buyers to wait.

Update 2026-10-03 (FEAT-014, #164): the deadline went private too, on the same reasoning as the
drop schedule. A buyer who sees the day the seller must sell by can wait for it and bargain, and
that matters most on the car. What remains public is the reason ("I am relocating") and the
signals that need no date: no holds, sold items visible, real drops. Being honest means never
saying anything false. It does not mean publishing every true fact that weakens your position.

## Anti-Pattern (What NOT to do)
Invented demand ("3 people asking"), fake deadlines, or "prices go up tomorrow".

## Golden Rule (The Pattern)
> **Every urgency signal on the page must be a fact the buyer could verify.**
